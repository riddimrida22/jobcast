# Jobcast - Growth Plan

How the app gets popular. Companion to BUILD_PLAN.md. The method is the marketing: the tool only grows if
people trust it, and trust comes from the sourced, transparent model, not from claims.

## Positioning

The honest, sourced forecast for your job. Ranges, not verdicts. Every number shows its work. This framing
is both the ethical stance and the competitive moat: most "will AI take my job" sites are opaque novelties.

## Growth engine 1: programmatic SEO (biggest lever)

- Generate a public, indexable page for every US occupation: "Will AI replace [occupation]?" seeded from
  O*NET's ~900 occupations.
- Each page is a real, sourced mini-report (exposure, timeline, exposed vs protected tasks, adjacent
  roles), not a stub. This matters: Google penalises thin auto-generated content, so the defensible model
  is exactly what makes this play safe and durable.
- These pages rank for the long-tail queries people actually type, and each funnels to the personalised
  tool ("get your own read: add your zip, age, and pay").
- Metro variants later ("Will AI replace [occupation] in [metro]?") multiply the long tail once local data
  is wired.

## Growth engine 2: shareability loop

- Every result has a clean URL and an auto-generated Open Graph image: "My job is 34% AI-exposed. Check
  yours." Built for LinkedIn (the paying demographic), X, and Reddit.
- The share drives a new visitor to their own read, which produces another shareable card. That is the
  viral loop.

## Launch channels

- **Hacker News**: lead with the transparent-methodology angle ("we built an open, sourced job-automation
  model"). The method is the story that gets upvoted, not the gimmick.
- **Reddit**: r/jobs, r/careerguidance, r/cscareerquestions, and profession-specific subs, where the
  occupation pages answer real recurring questions.
- **Product Hunt**: one clean launch.
- **LinkedIn**: professional audience, the demographic most likely to pay for a mitigation plan.

## Growth engine 3: PR and rankings

- Publish rankings media will cite: "the 20 most and least AI-exposed jobs," refreshed each data cycle.
  Every outlet that cites it is a backlink and a traffic spike.
- The rankings double as a periodic "State of Job Automation" report that updates as the data refreshes,
  making Jobcast the citable authority rather than one more quiz.

## Conversion funnel (free -> paid)

1. Visitor lands (SEO page or share) -> reads the free occupation-level read.
2. Adds personal inputs (zip, age, pay) -> gets the personalised free read. This is the hook.
3. At the moment of concern, the upsell: "here is your risk; here is your plan to get ahead of it" ->
   paid mitigation plan.
- The upsell lands hardest right after a high-exposure result, so the paid CTA sits in context, not as a
  generic banner.

## Retention

- "Notify me when my score changes" captures email and brings people back when data or research updates
  (ties directly to the dynamic engine in BUILD_PLAN.md).
- A short periodic email: what changed in the data, what it means for subscribers' roles.

## Risks (manage from day one)

- **Ranges, not verdicts, everywhere.** "We told him his job was gone and it was not" is the one headline
  that could sink the brand. Every marketing claim and every result stays probabilistic.
- **Thin-content SEO penalty.** Programmatic pages must each be genuinely sourced and useful, or Google
  demotes them. The defensible model is the safeguard.
- **Sensitivity of the subject.** This touches livelihoods. Tone stays calm, practical, and oriented to
  "what to do," never alarmist.
- **Entity and privacy before money.** No paid tier or public launch until the LLC/P.C. question and a
  privacy policy are settled.

## Metrics to track

- Organic sessions and ranking occupation pages (SEO health).
- Share rate per result and visitors per share (viral coefficient).
- Free-read completion rate and free -> paid conversion.
- Email capture rate and return visits on score-change notifications.
