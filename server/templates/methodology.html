The score blends four task-intrinsic signals: AI exposure (Eloundou et al. 2023), physical/robotics exposure (O*NET work context), how routine and structured the work is, and how interpersonal it is (the strongest protector in our backtest). The blend weights are set by that backtest, not guessed; the timeline then shifts for local demand (BLS OEWS), union coverage, and age.

What your 1 to 10 level means

Your headline is a resilience level from 1 to 10, where 10 is safest and 1 is most exposed. It comes straight from a relative exposure index (0 to 100), not a probability: an index of 80 means this job is more exposed to automation than 80% of the occupations we rate, which sits near the bottom of the scale (a low level). We flip and band that index into ten levels so the headline reads the intuitive way round — higher is safer.

The ranking behind it is the part we validate against real employment data (above); the level is simply a plain-English way to show where you sit in it. It does not say your personal chance of losing your job, which is why we show it as a range, not a verdict. The two dials below — AI/software and physical/robotics — are the raw component exposures that feed the blend.

What we never use

Gender, race, disability, and other protected characteristics are never inputs to the rating. Age is used only to work out your remaining working years.
