Why AI data centres are so big — and the levers that could make the compute curve flatter, greener, and partly distributed.

The question

Do the campuses really need to be that big?

Not inherently. "Big" is driven by training and by where power is available — not by physics.

  • A large, growing share of load is flexible — inference, batch, fault-tolerant training
  • It's distributable in space, shiftable in time, or both
  • The blocker isn't user capacity — it's coupling, verification, weight security, job state

The core insight

Compute is perishable inventory

An idle GPU-second is gone forever — like a flown seat or an unbooked room.

Expedia / Hotwire / Priceline monetised exactly this: sell the perishable unit cheap, on worse terms, without undercutting the full-price tier.

Any price above marginal energy cost beats the zero you get from letting the second evaporate.

Four levers

Ways to flatten & distribute the curve

01 · SPACE

Local inference

Small models on the user's own silicon. Strongest case: privacy — secrets never leave the device.

02 · MARKET

Compute buyback

Sell spare GPU time into a pool, like utility solar net-metering. Supply self-scales with demand.

03 · SPACE

Follow-the-sun

Route each query to an awake region; pour flexible work into the regions that are asleep.

04 · TIME

Demand-shaping

Grid playbook — peak-shave, valley-fill, load-shift into cheap, green, cool off-peak windows.

Reality check

This market already exists — partly

  • Cloud spot markets — AWS Spot, GCP Preemptible, Azure Spot: unsold capacity at 60–90% off, revocable. The airline model, literally.
  • GPU broker marketplaces — Vast.ai, RunPod, SF Compute, Together, Lambda. "Expedia for GPUs."
  • Decentralised training — Prime Intellect (INTELLECT-1), Nous, Gensyn, Bittensor, HF Petals. DiLoCo/DeMo loosen the coupling wall.
  • Carbon/demand-aware scheduling — hyperscalers already time-shift to green hours; datacentres sign grid demand-response.

Chain is real: datacentre spot → broker marketplace → individual contributors. The last link is the frontier.

Be honest

The hard walls

  • Network coupling — home links ~1000× too slow for all-to-all gradient sync
  • Verification — did the paid node really do the work, or return plausible garbage?
  • Weight security — training on a stranger's machine means shipping them the crown jewels
  • Job state / preemption — only interruption-tolerant work suits perishable inventory
  • Efficiency vs flexibility — a packed cluster wins cost-per-FLOP; distributed wins resilience, locality, grid impact

These flatten and green the curve. They do not make the baseline training campuses disappear.

Demand-shaping SaaS

Vertical / regional brokers

Aggregate perishable inventory for a buyer segment the generalists serve badly.

Monetisation

Ways to make money (crowded → open)

  • 1 · Perishable-inventory brokerage — take a cut; crowded, only a verticalised wedge
  • 2 · Spot-arbitrage + checkpointing-as-a-service — resell preemptible as "reliable"; margin = reliability
  • 3 · Privacy-first edge inference SDK/router — subscription; highest defensibility ⭐
  • 4 · Carbon/cost-aware scheduler — priced as a share of savings; low capital ⭐
  • 5 · Verification / trust middleware — proof-of-compute, reputation; strong moat if it works
  • 6 · Consumer GPU buyback — platform fee per matched hour; needs verification solved first
  • 7 · Grid demand-response aggregation — sell flexibility to utilities; capital- & relationship-heavy

Recommendation

Start with the low-capital, defensible wedge

Privacy-first hybrid inference (#3) or a savings-priced scheduler (#4).

  • Lowest capital — spend on learning, not GPUs
  • A buyer you can name and email — one regulated vertical
  • Defensible & values-aligned — rhymes with an honest, privacy-respecting brand

The honest take

What's true, and what's next

True: users have the capacity; inference buyback works today; the cycles are real and already exploited.

Limit: network coupling, verification, and weight security bound how far it goes — the baseline campuses don't vanish.

Next: pick one wedge, run 10 interviews, ship a landing page. Let paying interest decide.

All market claims are a starting map to verify before any pitch or decision.
